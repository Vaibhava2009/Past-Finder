A short history

Hampi, India

A selected introduction to this extraordinary former capital.

Read the full source ↗
Founded / established c. 1336

It rose as the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire.

Languages spoken Kannada & Telugu

Languages closely associated with the historical region.

Famous local dish Bisi bele bath

A beloved rice-and-lentil dish from Karnataka.

Population 2,133

Recorded population of Hampi village in the 2011 Census.

Area 10,250 acres

Approximate area of the Hampi heritage landscape.

14th century

Hampi became the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, a powerful South Indian kingdom that prospered for more than two centuries.

15th–16th c.

At its height, the city was one of the world’s largest and wealthiest urban centres, celebrated for its markets, temples and monumental stone architecture.

1565

After the Battle of Talikota, Vijayanagara was defeated. The city was subsequently sacked and largely abandoned.

1986

The Group of Monuments at Hampi was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognising its exceptional archaeological and cultural value.

Why it matters

Stones that remember

Hampi’s surviving landscape brings together royal, sacred and everyday life from the height of the Vijayanagara Empire.
